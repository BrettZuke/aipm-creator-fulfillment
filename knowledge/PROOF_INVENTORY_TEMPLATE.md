# Proof Inventory

What this client can actually put on camera. The writer's third input, alongside the Brain (how
content is made) and the signal (what is working right now).

Why it exists: without it the writer produces "I help coaches grow", which any account could say.
With it the writer produces the specific screen, the specific number of steps, the specific thing
that broke. Specificity is the whole difference between this client's content and generic AI
content, and it cannot be invented, so it has to be written down.

**Rule for everything in this file: it must be real and showable.** If the client cannot pull it up
on screen or tell the story truthfully, it does not belong here. No invented results, no revenue
figures that cannot be evidenced, no claims that would need a receipt the client does not have. The
writer will use anything in here as fact.

**Every `##` heading is one subject in the rotation.** The machine picks whichever subject has gone
longest without being made, so a thin file repeats itself and a fat one does not. Adding a heading
is the cheapest way to widen what the machine can talk about. Each run is handed only its own
section, because neighbouring sections bleed into each other and produce the same video twice.

Fill this in during phase 1, from the onboarding call and the transcripts. Load it with:

```bash
python3 tools/content-machine/load_knowledge_doc.py \
  --agency <workspace id> --title "Proof Inventory" --file knowledge/PROOF_INVENTORY.md
```

## THE ORIGIN, WHICH THEY CAN TELL COLD

The story before the business. Where they started, what it cost, the moment it turned. This is the
one section that never goes stale and never needs updating, so it carries the account on the days
there is nothing new to show.

### [The before]
Where they were, concretely. The job, the town, the number in the bank. What it felt like, in their
own words from the onboarding call, not paraphrased into something tidier.

### [The turn]
What actually changed, and what triggered it. Not the lesson, the event.

### [The after, told honestly]
Where they are now, only as far as they can evidence. If a number cannot be shown, cut it.

## WHAT THEY BUILT AND CAN SCREEN-RECORD

Anything the client can open on a screen while the camera runs. These make the strongest content
because they cannot be faked and nobody else has the same footage.

### [Thing one]
What it is in one line. Then the specifics: how many steps, what it replaced, what it costs to run,
what it looked like before. Name the screens worth recording.

## THINGS THAT BROKE, AND WHAT THEY LEARNED

Failure stories, each one true. These outperform wins in almost every account because they are the
only content a competitor cannot copy. One heading per story, with what broke, why, and what they
do differently now.

## THE PHILOSOPHY, IN THEIR OWN WORDS

The handful of opinions they will repeat for years. Take these verbatim from the transcripts: a
belief rewritten in your words stops sounding like them, which is the single most common way this
file goes wrong.

## CLAIMS APPROVED FOR USE

The exact wording of anything numeric or verifiable, written once, here, so the writer never has to
guess. Anything not on this list must not appear as fact in a script.

- [claim, in the exact words that are true]

## WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT HERE

Topics the client does not want to talk about, claims they will not make, and formats they have
ruled out. Write the reason next to each, because a rule without a reason gets argued with later.
