# Who this is for

One paragraph. Copy this file to `WHO.md` and fill it in during phase 1, from the onboarding
call and the transcripts. The scripter and the competitor-email reader both read it, so an
empty or vague one produces content that could have been written about anyone.

Answer five things, in the client's own framing rather than yours:

1. **Their name and handle**, as they would appear on screen.
2. **What they actually do.** The mechanism, not the category. "Builds websites" is a
   category; "builds one-page sites for trades in a weekend" is a mechanism.
3. **Who for.** Specific enough that a stranger reading it knows whether it means them.
4. **What they are NOT.** This does more work than everything above it. It is what stops the
   machine writing guru content for someone who hates gurus.
5. **What their proof is.** The thing they can point at. Work, numbers they can evidence,
   people they have helped.

---

## Example of the shape (not the content)

> [Name], @[handle]. They build [the specific thing] for [the specific people], and get paid
> [how], so they are judged on [what]. They are not a [the adjacent thing they get mistaken
> for] and do not teach [the thing they refuse to teach]. Their proof is the work itself:
> [two or three concrete, showable things]. They talk like [the register], not like [the
> register they would hate].

Keep it to one paragraph. It gets prepended to prompts, and a page of it crowds out the craft.
