# Sales Call Analyzer

Your audience tells you exactly what to post, on every sales and client call. They ask the same questions, raise the same objections, and reveal the same fears and false beliefs. Content built from those exact words converts, because it answers what is actually stopping people from buying, in language they already use.

This skill reads a call and turns the buyer's words into content.

## Step 1 — Get the transcript

Accept whatever the user has: a pasted transcript, a text/markdown/Word file, a doc, or notes. If they only have audio or a recording link, ask them to provide a transcript (most meeting tools export one). If the transcript has speaker labels, note which speaker is the prospect/client vs the user, you care most about the buyer's words.

If they paste several calls, that is even better, patterns across calls are stronger signal.

## Step 2 — Extract the raw signal

Read the call and pull out, in the buyer's own words wherever possible:

- **Questions they asked** (FAQs): anything they needed clarified or were unsure about.
- **Objections**: reasons to hesitate, delay, or not buy ("too expensive," "no time," "tried it before," "need to think").
- **Limiting beliefs**: false or limiting things they believe about themselves or the path ("I'm not technical," "it only works if you're already big," "passive income is a scam").
- **Pain and desire**: the problem in their words, and the outcome they want.
- **Language and phrases**: exact words, metaphors, and phrasing they used. This is gold for hooks.
- **Moments of relief or excitement**: where they leaned in or said "oh that makes sense", those are your strongest angles.

Capture these as raw quotes or close paraphrases. Don't polish yet.

## Step 3 — Turn each one into a content idea

Deliver **content ideas only** — the source, a hook, the format, and the angle. Do **not** write full posts, captions, scripts, or carousels here; this skill finds the ideas, the user (or another skill like Idea Hacker) writes them up. Keeping it to ideas means the user gets a whole backlog of angles from one call instead of one finished post.

For each meaningful item, write a content idea that answers or reframes it. Don't just list the objection, give the user something they can build from. Each idea should include:

- **The source** (the question/objection/belief it came from)
- **A hook** in the buyer's language
- **The format** that fits best (reel, carousel, email, story, short)
- **One line on the angle** (what the piece actually argues)

Group the ideas by theme so the user can see their content pillars emerge. Use this structure:

```
# Content from [call name/date]

## What your buyer actually said
- [3-6 of the most telling quotes, lightly cleaned]

## Content ideas

### Objection-killers
1. Source: "[objection]" → Hook: "[hook in their words]" — Format: [x] — Angle: [one line]
2. ...

### FAQ content (the questions they always ask)
1. ...

### Belief-shift content (the false beliefs to break)
1. ...

## This week's picks
The 3 ideas I'd post first, and why (usually the most-repeated objection and the most emotional belief).
```

## Why this works (keep this in mind while writing)

The goal is not clever content, it is *resonant* content. When a stranger sees a post that names the exact objection in their head, in the words they would use, they feel understood, and understood is the first step to trusted. So stay close to the buyer's actual language. The more it sounds like their own thought read back to them, the better it performs. Avoid generalizing the objections into bland marketing speak.

## Privacy note

Call content is sensitive. Keep client names, company names, and identifying details out of the content ideas themselves, the ideas should be general enough to post publicly without exposing who said what. The user's own numbers and stories are fine; the other person's identity is not.
